## summary

`orchestrator.py` grew to 4,198 lines, 47 imports, and 23 public methods through two months of reasonable incremental additions. bernstein decomposed it into 15 focused sub-packages using a YAML plan with dependency edges executed by 11 parallel agents - about three hours wall-clock versus an estimated two to three human days. the decomposition used a hard rule of 600 lines as the ceiling for any single module; the largest sub-package after the split was `agents/` at ~380 lines. the original file became a ~200-line re-export shim so that `from bernstein.core.orchestrator import Orchestrator` kept working in all existing scripts. later refactors took the count from 15 to 22 sub-packages by mid-April.

## key takeaways

- 11 parallel agents extracted 15 sub-packages in ~3 hours wall-clock; each sub-package is under 380 lines and averages ~280.
- the `orchestrator.py` shim kept all existing import paths working - backward compatibility was non-negotiable.
- the plan had explicit dependency edges (`tasks/` before `agents/`) because the merge queue calls task-completion callbacks; extracting in the wrong order would have produced circular imports.
- the test suite caught 14 import errors, 3 circular imports, and 1 module-level state bug during the decomposition - the safety net that made parallelism viable.
